# THE DIGITAL MARKETING OVERLAY — American and European industries

*Physician, heal thyself. The people who build everyone else's marketing leave the biggest footprints.*

Read this file whenever the target is an **agency** (SEO, PPC, social, web design, CRO, PR, ORM),
an **SEO/PPC shop or freelancer collective**, a **martech or SaaS vendor**, a **review, directory or
lead-gen platform**, an **influencer or affiliate network** — or any business whose site carries an
agency badge (which, for the local-service and legal verticals this skill sees most, is nearly always).
It extends three movements at once: the Satchel (claims, outreach, tracking, information duties — rows
in the Codex format: **Rule · Cite · Observable test · Ceiling · Route**), Justice (agency Pattern
Verdict axes and footprint sources) and the Reckoning (what "built by an agency" means for the audit).
Posture rule from the Satchel holds: **competitor/market** findings ship with a route and an evidentiary
bar; **self-audit** findings ship as remediation tickets with an owner — and an agency auditing itself
uses §8 of this file first.

**Cite status.** Every cite in this file is **◐** — drafted 2026-09-22 from memory, not fetched — until a
run fetches the live text and upgrades it to ✔ in the workbook (Satchel law 5). No cite here appears in a
CONFIRMED finding until that fetch is on record with its date. Section numbers of EU national statutes
change with every reform; where this file gives a section, treat it as the place to start reading, not
the thing to quote.

**Jurisdiction control, restated for this industry.** An American agency selling to American clients is
bound by US law and by the platforms; it picks up GDPR (Art. 3(2)) the moment it targets or monitors EU
users — including by cold-emailing EU prospects — and it picks up the national marketing rules of every
EU country it markets *into*. A European agency selling into the US is inside FTC and state UDAP reach
for its US-facing claims. Decide which rows bind before writing any of them up.

## 1 · The agency surface — what is observable from here

An agency shows the public more than its clients do, because its product *is* marketing:

- **Its own site:** partner badges (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Shopify, HubSpot, Semrush, Klaviyo, Amazon
  Ads), award badges and "Top 10 / Best of" plaques, "as seen on" media strips, client logo walls,
  testimonials and case studies with numbers, guarantees and pricing pages, "free audit / free website"
  offers, careers pages with pay ranges (or not), an Impressum / mentions légales / company-number footer
  (or not), a cookie banner (or not), a chatbot or "AI receptionist" (disclosed or not), the tag stack
  it runs on itself.
- **Its footprint:** the badge text and link it puts in every client footer (Justice Phase 1), the
  hosting and DNS signature (Justice Phase 1.4), the RDAP registrant on client domains, the shared GTM
  container / GA4 property / call-tracking / chat / review-widget keys across its book (§7), the schema
  templates it ships, the duplicate paragraphs it ships (quoted-sentence search).
- **Its outreach:** sending and look-alike domains (RDAP registration dates), SPF / DKIM / DMARC posture
  (DNS TXT, observable), landing pages for cold campaigns, LinkedIn automation tells, "we called from
  Google" scripts reported on consumer forums (hearsay; context only).
- **Its listings:** Google Business Profile(s) — agencies are the classic multi-city virtual-office
  offenders (listing-integrity §3, §9); Clutch / GoodFirms / DesignRush / UpCity / Sortlist / G2 / Trustpilot
  profiles and how the reviews got there; the Google Partners, Meta Business Partners and platform partner
  directories where a badge either is or is not.
- **Its regulated clients:** the claims an agency writes for lawyers, clinics, lenders and contractors
  bind the *client* under the Codex overlays — and in several places bind the agency too (§4).

What is *not* observable stays on the NOT OBSERVABLE tab: ad spend, client contracts, retainer terms,
who actually wrote what, the agency's internal review-solicitation practice, and whether an outreach
list was scraped. Infer nothing across those gaps without naming the inference.

jurisdiction. B2B claims in the EU fall under the **Misleading and Comparative Advertising Directive

## 7 · Agency Pattern Verdict axes (Justice extension)

Compute prevalence across the trial set exactly as Justice Phase 7 does (≥ 40 % of sites = platform-level).
These axes are the ones that only exist because an agency built the sites:

1. **Shared measurement layer.** The same GTM container ID, GA4 measurement ID, call-tracking account,
   chat key or review-widget key on ≥ 2 unrelated client sites (`scripts/satchel_web.py scan` lists them;
   compare across the book). Finding: agency-owned container → data co-mingling across clients, client
   cannot leave with their data, one compromised container reaches every client. Rule hooks: GDPR
   Art. 28 / CCPA §7051 (K); platform terms (K).
2. **Domain and DNS ownership.** RDAP registrant / admin = the agency; nameservers = the agency's; the
   client's email on the agency's mail platform. Finding: lock-in; a "you own your website" claim on the
   agency's site turns this C-capable (§3).
3. **Footer badge link scheme.** Every client footer links to the agency with an optimised anchor
   ("Dallas SEO Company", "Web Design by [Agency] | SEO Services"). Finding: Google link-spam exposure for
   the *clients* (widely distributed footer links) and a manipulated profile for the agency. Fix: brand
   anchor, `rel="nofollow"` or `sponsored`, one line. Observable in one fetch per client.
4. **Duplicate copy across the book.** A quoted 12-word sentence from one client's service page searched
   in quotes returns other clients. Finding: scaled templated content; doorway risk compounds when the
   pages are city pages.
5. **Doorway templates.** `site:<client> intitle:"<city>" <service>` volume × identical H1 templates across
   clients.
6. **Schema templates.** The agency's own Organization / sameAs / logo left in client JSON-LD; the wrong
   `@type` for the trade on every site; self-serving `aggregateRating` shipped platform-wide (lying schema
   ×N).
7. **Consent posture.** EU-facing clients with pre-consent firing on every site the agency built (§5) —
   one fix at the template level clears the book.
8. **Speed profile.** The same unused-JS bundle, hero video pattern or font-weight sprawl across sites
   (Justice Phase 3: platform defect vs site defect).
9. **Accessibility posture.** axe-core across the book; decisive when the agency sells compliance.
10. **Review-widget provenance.** The same third-party widget injecting reviews client-side ("Loading
    reviews…") on every site — invisible to crawlers, and the same review counts appearing on unrelated
    businesses is a provenance flag (CANDIDATE ceiling; never "fake").
11. **Claims uniformity.** The same "#1", "guaranteed", "award-winning" copy on multiple client sites =
    agency-written template claims; the exposure cascades to every client at once (§2 rows apply per
    site; the Pattern Verdict names the template).
12. **Outreach infrastructure.** Look-alike sending domains (RDAP < 90 days, no DMARC, no site) tied to
    the agency by nameserver or registrant.

Present the systemic list first (prevalence × severity), two example URLs each, the fix an implementer
would apply at the template level, then the strengths the platform reliably delivers, then the
opening — framed as opportunity, never as a smear, never as a guaranteed outcome.

## 8 · Self-audit: an agency's own house (posture = self-audit; tickets, owners, no routes)

Run these before anything else when the operator audits itself or a friendly agency:

1. Legal-notice / Impressum page reachable from every page with every mandatory item for each EU/UK
   market the agency markets into; US: physical postal address in email footers (CAN-SPAM).
2. Cookie consent: CMP present, reject as easy as accept, Consent Mode v2 wired, nothing fires
   pre-consent on EU-facing pages (render probe transcript on file).
3. Partner badges: each one verifiable in the program directory today; tier wording matches; no
   "Google certified agency"; no "Google Guaranteed".
4. Awards and "as seen on": each with a conferring body, a methodology and a date; paid memberships and
   syndication labelled as such or removed.
5. Testimonials and case studies: material connections disclosed; results claims carry a timeframe and a
   basis; no insiders; no review gating anywhere in the funnel; no sentiment-conditioned incentives.
6. Guarantees and "free" offers: conditions on the offer page; refund terms stated.
7. Outreach: SPF / DKIM / DMARC on every sending domain; one-click unsubscribe; opt-in posture for DE / AT /
   IT / ES lists; Art. 14 notice for sourced data; TPS / CTPS screening for UK calls; PEWC for US texts.
8. AI agents (chatbots, receptionists — the operator's own product line): disclosure at first
   interaction on every deployment; a truthful answer to "are you a bot"; Art. 50 posture ready for
   EU-facing deployments; UT / CA thresholds noted for US ones.
9. Careers pages: pay ranges where required; no citizenship-only language without a legal basis.
10. Client book: no shared GTM container unless documented in the processor agreement; brand-anchor
    footer credits; no agency schema on client sites; client owns domain, DNS, analytics and ad accounts,
    and the contract says so.

## 9 · Observable-test cookbook for this industry

- **Footprint phrases:** `"Powered by <Agency>"`, `"Site by"`, `"Web Design by"`, `"Marketing by"`,
  `"Digital Marketing by"`, `"SEO by"`, `"Website by"`, `"Designed by"`, `"Built by"`, always
  `-site:<agency.tld>`, crossed with the agency's verticals and cities (Justice Phase 1.2).
- **Badge verification:** Google Partners directory; Meta Business Partners directory; Microsoft
  Advertising partner directory; Shopify Partners / Experts; HubSpot Solutions Partner directory; Klaviyo
  partner directory; Semrush Agency Partners — search the program name plus "directory" for the current
  URL; screenshot the result with the date. Absence in a directory is a null result — apply the taxonomy
  (name variant, lapsed tier, directory lag) before writing anything.
- **Awards:** the conferring body's own methodology page; the platform's advertising-disclosure page;
  the award's date (a 2019 plaque on a 2026 homepage is stale, not false).
- **Reviews:** the profile URL on each platform with its review count and date; velocity from the dates
  visible; employee cross-check via the agency's team page and LinkedIn (labelled inferred).
- **Ownership and infrastructure:** `https://rdap.org/domain/<domain>` (registrant org, dates, nameservers);
  `dig TXT <domain>` / `_dmarc.<domain>` (SPF, DMARC); crt.sh for look-alikes and staging hosts (`?q=%25<name>%25&output=json`);
  Wayback availability for tenure and expired-domain reuse.
- **Shared layers:** `scripts/satchel_web.py scan` on 5–12 client homepages → compare `gtm_ids`,
  `ga4_ids`, `pixel_ids`, chat and call-tracking keys, review-widget hosts.
- **Duplicate copy:** a quoted 12-word sentence from a service page, searched; a quoted testimonial.
- **Legal notice:** fetch `/impressum`, `/mentions-legales`, `/aviso-legal`, `/legal`, `/note-legali`,
  `/colofon`, `/imprint`, plus the footer links; check every mandatory item.
- **Consent:** `scripts/satchel_render.py` on the homepage and a money page with an EU locale header;
  pre-consent request log is the evidence.
- **AI agents:** render-probe transcript: open the chat, ask "are you a person?", record the answer.

## 10 · What "built by an agency" changes in a Reckoning of any domain

- Read the footer credit first (Phase 2) and record the agency of record. Their platform's known
  defects (a prior Justice run, or this file's §7 axes) become the first hypotheses to test — not
  conclusions.
- Distinguish **inherited** findings (template, tag stack, hosting, schema, footer link scheme — fix at
  the agency or by leaving) from **owned** findings (content, reviews, citations, GBP) in the roadmap;
  the owner reads the difference as "what I can fix Monday" versus "what I need to renegotiate".
- Never write "your agency did this to you". Write what the artifact displays, resolves to, or loads,
  and where the fix lives.
- Offer the reverse Justice run in one line: "the same footer credit appears on N other sites; a
  platform-level review would tell you whether these defects are yours or the template's."
