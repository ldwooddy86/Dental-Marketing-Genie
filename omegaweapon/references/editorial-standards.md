# Editorial Standards — the Pass 4 playbook

Read this before your first rewrite in a conversation.

The goal is copy that reads like a sharp human wrote it for a worried person, not
copy that scores perfectly. Someone reading a personal injury page at 2 a.m. after
a car accident is scared, possibly in pain, and probably on a phone. Every extra
clause costs you.

**The one rule that overrides everything here: simplify the sentence, never the
law.** Losing a qualifier is not an editorial improvement, it is a legal error.

---

## Order of operations

Structure before sentences before words. Fixing structure first makes the
sentence-level work smaller, and fixing words first means redoing it.

1. Bullets back to prose
2. Split very-hard sentences
3. Passive to active
4. AI vocabulary out
5. Typos and grammar sweep

---

## 1. Bullets back to prose

Bullet bloat is the most recognizable AI signature in this genre, and it is
structural, so no amount of word-swapping hides it.

**Keep a list when** the content is genuinely enumerable and the reader will scan
rather than read: documents to bring, filing deadlines, categories of damages,
steps in a process.

**Convert to prose when** the bullets are full sentences, when they are arguments
rather than items, or when the list is a rhetorical device rather than a
structure. A "Why Choose Us" list of seven bold-lead-in bullets is the canonical
case.

**Kill bold-lead-in bullets on sight.** `**Proven Results:** We've recovered over
$250 million` is the single loudest tell in law firm copy. So are fragment
bullets — five words, no punctuation, no verb.

> **Before**
> - **Proven Results:** We've recovered over $250 million for our clients
> - **No Fee Unless We Win:** You pay nothing unless we recover
> - **Free Case Review:** Available 24/7
> - **Experienced:** Over 40 years of combined experience
>
> **After**
> Our attorneys have handled injury claims in South Florida for more than 40
> years, and we have recovered over $250 million for clients. We work on
> contingency, so you owe no attorney's fee unless we recover for you, though you
> may still be responsible for case costs. Case reviews are free and we answer
> the phone at any hour.

Note what happened besides the format change: the contingency line picked up its
required cost disclosure, and "Proven Results" — an unverifiable characterization
— became a factual statement. Structural editing is often where compliance fixes
land naturally.

**Budget:** no more than 30% of the document's words inside bullets, and no run
longer than seven.

## 2. Split hard sentences

A sentence over about 25 words in consumer legal copy is usually two sentences
wearing a trench coat. The seams are almost always at `which`, `and`, `but`,
`because`, `so that`, `in order to`, or a semicolon.

> **Before (grade 15.5, 22 words)**
> The discovery rule may extend this period in certain limited circumstances where
> the injury was not immediately apparent to the injured party.
>
> **After (grade 8.4)**
> Sometimes an injury does not show up right away. In those cases, the discovery
> rule may give you more time.

Do not split a sentence in a way that separates a rule from its qualifier. "You
have two years to file" followed three sentences later by "unless the discovery
rule applies" reads as two independent claims, and the first one is wrong on its
own. Keep the qualifier adjacent, even if it costs you a grade point.

## 3. Passive to active

Legal writing defaults to passive, and it reads as evasive to someone who wants
to know whether *you* can get *them* money. It also hides the actor, which in
legal copy is often the whole point of the sentence.

| Passive | Active |
|---|---|
| Compensation may be recovered for medical expenses | You can recover your medical expenses |
| Your claim will be forever barred | You lose the right to file |
| Cases are handled on a contingency basis | We handle cases on contingency |
| Settlements are typically obtained within 6 to 12 months | Most cases settle in six to twelve months |
| Our attorneys are ready to be contacted by you | Call us |
| It was determined by the court that | The court found that |
| A lawsuit must be filed within two years | You must file within two years |
| Damages are awarded by the jury | The jury awards damages |

**When passive is correct — do not "fix" these:**

- The actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant: "The vehicle was manufactured in
  2019."
- The recipient is the subject the reader cares about: "You were injured through
  no fault of your own" is better than "Someone else's negligence injured you."
- Legal terms of art: "the case was remanded," "the statute was amended," "the
  rule was vacated." These are the conventional forms and rewriting them reads as
  wrong to a lawyer.

**Budget:** 10% of sentences or fewer.

## 4. AI vocabulary

Two categories, and the second is specific to this genre.

### General AI tells

delve into · navigating the complexities · in today's fast-paced world · it's
important to note · when it comes to · rest assured · leverage · utilize ·
seamless · robust · holistic · unlock · elevate · empower · embark on · a
testament to · plays a crucial role · in conclusion · furthermore · moreover ·
whether you're · look no further · peace of mind · tailored to your needs ·
cutting-edge · game-changer · first and foremost · last but not least

### Legal-marketing-specific tells

These are worse, because they are both machine-sounding *and* often bar-rule
problems:

fight tirelessly · fight for the compensation you deserve · aggressive
representation · we're here to help · don't go it alone · time is of the essence ·
the insurance company is not on your side · every case is unique · navigating the
legal system · our experienced team · our dedicated team · compassionate
representation · trusted advocate · by your side · you deserve

**"You deserve" and "we will fight for the compensation you deserve" are not just
tired — they edge toward a prediction of outcome.** Route them through
`bar-advertising.md` § 2.1 as well as rewriting them.

**The substitution principle:** replace a claim about the firm's character with a
fact about the firm's work. "Aggressive representation" says nothing. "We have
taken 31 cases to verdict since 2015" says something, and it is verifiable, which
is what the bar rules want anyway.

### Simpler words

utilize → use · commence → start · terminate → end · endeavor → try · facilitate
→ help · demonstrate → show · subsequently → later · approximately → about ·
sufficient → enough · numerous → many · obtain → get · provide → give · purchase
→ buy · assist → help · regarding → about · pursuant to → under · in accordance
with → under · notwithstanding → despite · aforementioned → this · compensation →
payment · initiate → start · ascertain → find out · expedite → speed up ·
necessitate → require · cognizant → aware

**Do not substitute inside a term of art.** "Compensatory damages" does not become
"payment damages." "Pursuant to Rule 26" can stay if the audience is lawyers. Read
the sentence, not the word list.

## 5. Grammar sweep

The script catches mechanics. These need eyes:

- **Subject-verb agreement**, especially across an intervening clause: "The
  statute of limitations for wrongful death claims *is* two years" (not *are*).
- **Comma splices.** Very common in AI-generated copy where an em dash was
  removed and replaced with a comma. Watch for these specifically after Pass 1.
- **its / it's · their / there / they're · your / you're · affect / effect ·
  principal / principle**
- **Misplaced modifiers:** "Injured in a crash, our attorneys can help you" — the
  attorneys were not injured.
- **Tense drift** within a section, especially in case-result narratives.
- **Hyphenated compound modifiers:** *no-fault insurance*, *18-wheeler accident*,
  *third-party claim*, *work-related injury*. Not hyphenated after the noun: "the
  injury was work related."
- **Numbers:** spell out one through nine in prose, use numerals for 10 and up.
  **Always numerals for legal figures, statute numbers, dollar amounts, and
  deadlines** — "you have 2 years" is more scannable than "two years" for the
  thing a reader most needs to see, and consistency with the rest of the page
  matters less than clarity on a deadline.
- **Case names** are italicized: *Loper Bright v. Raimondo*. If the source
  document has no italics, leave the plain text alone rather than introducing
  markup the CMS may not carry.

**Never "fix" as a typo:** case names, firm names, attorney names, Latin terms
(*res ipsa loquitur*, *pro se*, *voir dire*, *per stirpes*), or court names.
Verify every spelling suspect before changing it.

## Reading level: how to get to grade 10 without losing the law

Grade level is driven by two things only: words per sentence, and syllables per
word. You have more room on the second than you think, because legal copy is full
of long words that *have* to stay.

**What to cut first:**

1. **Sentence length.** Splitting is nearly free and does most of the work.
2. **Prepositional chains.** "In the event of the occurrence of an accident" →
   "If you're in an accident."
3. **Nominalizations.** "Make a determination" → "decide." "Provide assistance" →
   "help." "Give consideration to" → "consider."
4. **Redundant pairs.** "Null and void," "each and every," "full and complete" —
   legal drafting habits that add syllables and no meaning in marketing copy.

**What to leave alone:**

- Statutory terms, cause-of-action names, and court names.
- Any word whose replacement narrows or broadens the claim. "May" is not "will."
  "Generally" is not nothing. "Up to" is not "at least."
- Required disclaimer language, which is often deliberately formal and sometimes
  compelled verbatim.

If a section cannot reach grade 10 without stripping a qualifier, **leave the
qualifier and report the residual.** Say so plainly in the change log: "The
limitations section reads at grade 11.2. Bringing it lower would require dropping
the discovery-rule qualifier, which would make the statement inaccurate."

That is the right trade every time, and saying it out loud is what makes the rest
of the report credible.

---

## Knowing when to stop

Two editorial iterations maximum. Signs you have gone too far:

- Every sentence is the same length.
- The copy no longer sounds like the firm — a plaintiff's trial firm and an
  estate planning boutique should not read identically.
- You are rewriting sentences that were already fine to move a decimal point.
- You have started editing quoted material, testimonials, or attorney bios.

A page at grade 10.4 with a voice is better than a page at grade 8.9 without one.
Report the number and move on.
