## 1. Overruled cases and dead doctrines

Treat any reference to these as presumptively wrong absent a post-decision
qualifier.

| Doctrine in the copy | Killed by | Date | What to say instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Chevron* deference | **Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo**, No. 22-451 ✅ | 6/28/2024 | Courts exercise independent judgment on statutory interpretation; *Skidmore* respect survives. Hits SSD, veterans, environmental, immigration, employment, healthcare-regulatory pages. |
| *Humphrey's Executor* / "independent agencies are insulated" | **Trump v. Slaughter**, No. 25-332 ✅ | 6/29/2026 | For-cause removal protection for FTC commissioners is unconstitutional; the President may remove at will. The Fed question was expressly reserved. |
| "Background circumstances" heightened burden for majority-group Title VII plaintiffs | **Ames v. Ohio Dept. of Youth Services**, [No. 23-1039](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/24pdf/23-1039_c0n2.pdf) (9-0) ✅ | 6/5/2025 | The *McDonnell Douglas* burden is identical regardless of the plaintiff's group. |
| "Materially adverse action" required for a Title VII transfer claim | **Muldrow v. City of St. Louis**, No. 22-193 ⚠️ | 4/17/2024 | Plaintiff need show only **some** harm to a term or condition of employment. |
| Fifth Circuit "moment of threat" rule | **Barnes v. Felix**, No. 23-1239 ✅ | 5/15/2025 | Excessive force is judged on the totality of the circumstances. |
| Non-consensual third-party releases in Ch. 11 | **Harrington v. Purdue Pharma**, No. 23-124 ⚠️ | 6/27/2024 | The Code does not authorize them. Hits mass-tort and bankruptcy pages. |
| "Last-mile drivers must arbitrate" / § 1 exemption requires crossing state lines | **Flowers Foods v. Brock**, No. 24-935 (9-0) ✅ | 5/28/2026 | An intrastate leg of an interstate journey qualifies for the FAA § 1 transportation-worker exemption. Big for gig-economy and driver-misclassification pages. |
| "Employers must prove an FLSA exemption by clear and convincing evidence" | **E.M.D. Sales v. Carrera**, No. 23-217 ⚠️ | 1/15/2025 | Preponderance standard. |
| "NLRB needs only reasonable cause for a § 10(j) injunction" | **Starbucks v. McKinney**, No. 23-367 ⚠️ | 6/13/2024 | Traditional four-factor *Winter* test. |
| "You can't challenge a regulation more than six years after issuance" | **Corner Post v. Board of Governors**, No. 22-1008 ⚠️ | 7/1/2024 | The § 2401(a) clock runs from plaintiff injury, not rule publication. |
| "SEC penalty actions are heard by an ALJ" | **SEC v. Jarkesy**, No. 22-859 ⚠️ | 6/27/2024 | Seventh Amendment jury right attaches. |
| "Retirees can sue under ADA Title I over retiree benefits" | **Stanley v. City of Sanford**, No. 23-997 ⚠️ | 6/20/2025 | Generally not, if they neither hold nor seek a job at the time. |
| "ERISA plaintiffs must plead around § 1108 exemptions" | **Cunningham v. Cornell**, No. 23-1007 ⚠️ | 4/17/2025 | Exemptions are affirmative defenses the defendant pleads and proves. |
| State affidavit-of-merit requirements stated flatly | **Berk v. Choy**, No. 24-440 ✅ | 1/20/2026 | There is a federal-court carve-out. High-impact for med-mal pages. |
| Broker FAAAA preemption in trucking | **Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II**, No. 24-1238 ✅ | 5/14/2026 | High-impact trucking item — verify the holding before restating it. |
| Roundup/glyphosate failure-to-warn framing | **Monsanto v. Durnell**, No. 24-1068 ✅ | 6/25/2026 | Highest-impact mass-tort item of the period. Verify before restating. |
| "Nationwide injunction" framing | **Trump v. CASA**, No. 24A884 ⚠️ | 6/27/2025 | Universal-injunction practice changed. |
| Birthright citizenship "in jeopardy" | **Trump v. Barbara**, No. 25-365 ✅ | 6/30/2026 | EO 14160 was struck down. Highest-impact immigration item. |
| Geofence warrant framing | **Chatrie v. United States**, No. 25-112 ✅ | 6/29/2026 | High-impact criminal defense item. |

**A caution on citation format.** U.S. Reports volume numbers for opinions issued
after roughly March 2026 are ❓ unassigned or in flux. Docket number plus decision
date are the reliable identifiers. Do not "correct" a citation to a volume/page
you cannot verify.

## 2. Rules vacated, revoked, or abandoned

The distinction that trips people up: **"the rule was struck down" is different
from "the rule never took effect."** The FTC noncompete rule and the FTC
click-to-cancel rule were both vacated *before* their compliance dates. Copy
saying employers "had to comply" is wrong on the facts, not merely stale.

| Rule | What happened | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **FTC Non-Compete Clause Rule** (16 CFR 910) | Set aside nationwide Aug. 2024 (*Ryan LLC v. FTC*, N.D. Tex.) two weeks before its 9/4/2024 effective date. FTC voted 3-1 on 9/5/2025 to dismiss appeals and accede to vacatur; removed from the CFR. Enforceability is entirely state law now. | ✅ Dead. Highest-frequency employment-law error online. |
| **DOL 2024 Overtime Rule** | Vacated 11/15/2024 (E.D. Tex.); DOL removed the language from the CFR 5/14–15/2026. | ✅ Current threshold is **$684/week ($35,568)**, HCE **$107,432**. The automatic triennial update is gone. |
| **CTA / FinCEN beneficial ownership reporting** | FinCEN finalized 8/11/2026: **all U.S.-created entities are exempt**; no BOI for U.S.-person beneficial owners or company applicants. Only certain foreign entities file. | ✅ Domestic BOI-filing service pages are dead offerings. |
| **CFPB Medical Debt Credit Reporting Rule** | Vacated July 2025 (E.D. Tex.); court also held FCRA preempts contrary state laws. | ⚠️ Medical debt is federally reportable again. |
| **FTC Click-to-Cancel / Negative Option Rule** | Vacated by the Eighth Circuit 7/8/2025 days before compliance. FTC restarted rulemaking (signaled Feb. 2026). ROSCA enforcement continues. | ⚠️ |
| **EEOC PWFA rule — elective abortion accommodation** | That provision vacated May 2025 (*Louisiana v. EEOC*, W.D. La.). **The rest of the PWFA rule stands.** | ⚠️ |
| **EEOC 2024 Harassment Guidance — gender identity sections** | Vacated May 2025 (N.D. Tex.); EEOC formally rescinded those sections (Jan. 2026). Other sections remain. | ⚠️ |
| **DOL 2024 Independent Contractor Rule** (29 CFR 795) | Enforcement paused May 2025 (FAB 2025-1); NPRM published 2/27/2026 to rescind and restore a core-factors test. **The 2024 rule technically remains on the books and can still be cited in private litigation.** | ✅ Say "in regulatory limbo," not "repealed." |
| **EO 14026** federal contractor minimum wage ($17.75) | Revoked by EO 14236, 3/14/2025. DOL no longer enforces it or 29 CFR part 23. | ✅ EO 13658 still covers older contracts: **$13.65 / $9.55 tipped**, eff. **5/11/2026** (no 1/1/2026 change). |

**Agencies that changed or no longer exist.** "INS" (abolished 2003 → USCIS/ICE/CBP);
SSA's "ODAR" (renamed Office of Hearings Operations, 2017). Also: the CFPB has
undergone sustained defunding attempts, reorganization, and staffing cuts through
2025–2026 ⚠️ — flag copy presenting CFPB complaint handling as a dependable remedy
with a service-level promise.

## 3. 2026 federal figure tables

Every one of these is a *dated* claim. Any bare figure without an as-of year is
presumptively stale.

### Estate, gift, and income tax ✅
Source: [Rev. Proc. 2025-32 / IRS](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill)

| Item | **2026** | 2025 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Estate/gift basic exclusion | **$15,000,000** | $13,990,000 | $13,610,000 |
| Annual gift exclusion | **$19,000** | $19,000 | $18,000 |
| Gifts to non-citizen spouse | **$194,000** | $190,000 | $185,000 |
| Standard deduction MFJ / single / HoH | **$32,200 / $16,100 / $24,150** | — | — |

> **The single most widespread stale claim in estate-planning marketing.** Copy
> written 2018–2025 says the TCJA exclusion "sunsets January 1, 2026" and drops
> "to about $7 million," urging clients to act before then. **It did not happen.**
> The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (P.L. 119-21, 7/4/2025) set 2026 at $15,000,000,
> indexed. Any page containing "2026 sunset" or "use it or lose it" is flatly wrong.
>
> Related: portability election via the Rev. Proc. 2022-32 simplified method is
> **5 years** from date of death, not 2 ⚠️.

### Social Security / SSDI ✅
[SSA 2026 COLA](https://www.ssa.gov/news/en/cola/factsheets/2026.html) · [SGA table](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/sga.html)

COLA **2.8%** · SGA non-blind **$1,690/mo** (2025: $1,620 · 2024: $1,550) ·
SGA blind **$2,830** · trial work period **$1,210** · wage base **$184,500** ·
SSI individual **$994** / couple **$1,491** · max benefit at FRA **$4,152**

Common stale line: "if you earn more than $1,550 a month, you're not disabled" —
three years old.

### FLSA overtime ✅
**Current law: $684/week = $35,568/yr; HCE $107,432.** Special jurisdictions
often omitted: PR/Guam/USVI/CNMI **$455/week**; American Samoa **$380/week**;
motion picture **$1,043/week**; computer employees **$27.63/hour**.
Federal minimum wage remains **$7.25** (since 2009); tipped cash **$2.13** ⚠️.

### Immigration ✅
Base fee schedule (8 CFR 106.2, eff. 4/1/2024): I-130 **$675** · I-485 **$1,440** ·
I-765 **$520** ($260 with pending I-485) · N-400 **$760** · I-90 **$465** ·
I-751 **$750** · I-601A **$795**

Pre-April-2024 figures still on many sites — N-400 $640, I-130 $535, I-485 $1,140,
I-765 $410, I-751 $595 — are among the most commonly stale numbers on any law
firm website.

**New statutory fees (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21) — non-waivable, indexed:** asylum
application **$100** (previously free) plus a **$100/year Annual Asylum Fee**
while pending; EAD initial **$550** / renewal **$275**; SIJS **$250**; TPS
**$500**; humanitarian parole **$1,000**; asylum-based adjustment **$1,500**;
visa integrity fee **$250** minimum.

**EOIR fees, FY2026 (eff. 2/1/2026)** ✅: BIA appeal **$1,030** (was $110 — a 9x
change) · motion to reopen, IJ **$1,065** · EOIR-42A **$710** · EOIR-42B
**$1,640** (was $100) · adjustment in court **$2,980**.

**2026 HHS poverty guidelines** (48 states, eff. 1/13/2026) ✅: 1 **$15,960** ·
2 **$21,640** · 3 **$27,320** · 4 **$33,000** · each add'l **+$5,680**. I-864
sponsors need 125% → **$41,250** for a household of four.

### Medicare / CMS ✅
Part B premium **$202.90/mo** · Part B deductible **$283** · Part A inpatient
deductible **$1,736** · days 61–90 **$434/day** · SNF 21–100 **$217/day**

**Medicare Secondary Payer recovery threshold, CY2026: $750** for
physical-trauma-based liability, no-fault, and workers' comp — **unchanged**.
Does **not** apply to ingestion, implantation, or exposure claims. PI pages
commonly say $300 or $1,000, and almost never mention the carve-out.

### Bankruptcy ✅
Thresholds eff. **April 1, 2025**; next adjustment April 1, 2028.

Ch. 13 unsecured **$526,700** · secured **$1,580,125** · Subchapter V
**$3,424,000** · § 522(d)(1) homestead **$31,575** · vehicle **$5,025** ·
wildcard **$1,675 + $15,800** · § 507(a)(4) wage priority **$17,150** ·
preference threshold **$8,575**

> **Major trap.** The Bankruptcy Threshold Adjustment and Technical Corrections
> Act raised Subchapter V to **$7,500,000** and created a combined **$2,750,000**
> Chapter 13 limit. **Both sunset June 21, 2024** ⚠️. Pages written 2022–2024
> saying "up to $7.5 million in debt can use Subchapter V" are wrong by more than
> double, and any Chapter 13 page citing a single combined limit is wrong.

### Court fees
Federal civil: **$350** statutory + **$55** administrative = **$405** (schedule
eff. 12/1/2023, still current) ⚠️. "$402" is the pre-December-2023 figure and
still appears widely. Bankruptcy admin components ✅: Ch. 7 **$78** + **$15**
trustee surcharge; Ch. 11 **$571**. Commonly quoted totals (Ch. 7 $338, Ch. 13
$313, Ch. 11 $1,738) ⚠️.

### Unchanged — where the stale claim runs the other way
- **Title VII caps**, 42 U.S.C. § 1981a(b)(3): **$50,000 / $100,000 / $200,000 /
  $300,000** by employer size. Never indexed, unchanged since 1991. The error is
  copy asserting they "adjust for inflation." Back pay and front pay sit outside
  the cap, and § 1981 race claims are uncapped — omitting that is misleading
  rather than stale.
- **EEOC deadlines:** 180 days; 300 in deferral states; 45 days federal-sector.
- **FMLA:** 50 employees / 12 months / 1,250 hours / 50 within 75 miles / 12
  weeks. Unchanged since 1993 ⚠️. The error here is pages predicting federal
  paid-leave expansion or conflating FMLA with state PFL programs.

## 4. State-law changes with effective dates

Limitations and liability changes are almost never retroactive, so **the correct
statement is always two-part.** A single number is wrong for somebody no matter
which number you pick.

| State | Change | Effective | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FL** | Negligence SOL **4 years → 2 years** (HB 837) ⚠️ | 3/24/2023, prospective | Fla. Stat. § 95.11(5)(a): "WITHIN TWO YEARS — An action founded on negligence." Probably the highest-yield SOL check in the country. |
| **FL** | Pure → **modified comparative** negligence, 51% bar (non-med-mal) ⚠️ | 2023 | |
| **LA** | Delictual prescription **1 year → 2 years**, La. C.C. art. 3493.1 ✅ | 7/1/2024, prospective | Both a bare "one year" and a bare "two years" are wrong. |
| **LA** | Pure → **modified comparative fault**, 51% bar, art. 2323 ✅ | 1/1/2026, prospective | "You can recover even if you're 90% at fault" is now wrong for post-1/1/2026 accidents. |
| **CA** | MICRA cap replaced (AB 35) ⚠️ | escalating | **2026: $470,000** non-death / **$650,000** wrongful death; +$40k / +$50k per year to 2033, then indexed 2%. Pages still saying **$250,000** are decades out of date and still extremely common. |
| **CA** | Survival-action pre-death pain and suffering (CCP § 377.34) ⚠️ | **sunset 1/1/2026** | The SB 447 window covered actions filed 1/1/2022–1/1/2026. SB 29 to extend it failed. Flag any CA wrongful-death page written 2022–2025 promising this recovery. |
| **CO** | Noneconomic cap **$250,000 → $1,500,000** (HB24-1472) ✅ | 1/1/2025 | Wrongful death **$2,125,000**; med-mal phasing to $875,000 by 2030; indexing from 2028. Intermediate-year figures ❓ — pull the bill. |
| **MD** | Child Victims Act caps cut (SB 1) ✅ | claims filed on/after 6/1/2025 | **$1,500,000 → $700,000** private, **$890,000 → $400,000** public; "claim" replaced "incident or occurrence," so one cap per defendant regardless of the number of incidents. Pages advertising "up to $1.5 million per incident" are materially misleading to new clients. |
| **GA** | Tort reform SB 68 / SB 69 ✅ | 4/21/2025 mixed | Anchoring limits, bifurcation, voluntary-dismissal limits, single fee award — **immediate**. Phantom damages — **causes of action arising on/after 4/21/2025**. Litigation-funding registration — **1/1/2026**. Premises liability, seat-belt evidence, negligent security ❓ **not confirmed — do not assert.** |
| **NY** | Grieving Families Act **vetoed a fourth time** (S4423) ✅ | 12/5/2025 | Wrongful-death SOL remains 2 years; emotional-anguish damages remain unavailable. Flag copy saying it passed or is expected to. |
| **MD** | Child Victims Act (2023) eliminated the SOL for childhood sexual abuse claims ✅ | 2023 | |

## 5. Practice-area checklists

**Personal injury / mass tort** — Roundup failure-to-warn (*Monsanto v. Durnell*)
· broker liability in trucking (*Montgomery v. Caribe*) · government-contractor
preemption (*Hencely*) · med-mal affidavit of merit in federal court (*Berk v.
Choy*) · excessive force (*Barnes v. Felix*) · Medicare $750 threshold and the
exposure carve-out · IRS medical mileage · FL and LA SOL and comparative fault ·
CA MICRA, CO, MD caps · CA survival-action sunset · judicial estoppel for
undisclosed claims (*Keathley*)

**Employment** — FTC noncompete rule (dead) · overtime threshold ($684, not
$58,656) · *Ames* · *Muldrow* · FAA § 1 (*Flowers Foods*) · independent-contractor
test in limbo · PWFA abortion provision vacated · EEOC harassment guidance
gender-identity sections rescinded · Title VII caps unchanged

**Immigration** — birthright citizenship resolved (*Trump v. Barbara*) · **all
fee figures**, especially EOIR (BIA $110 → $1,030; EOIR-42B $100 → $1,640) ·
asylum is no longer free and carries a $100/year pending fee · I-864 poverty
figures · *Urias-Orellana* persecution standard · visa revocation unreviewable
(*Bouarfa*)

**Estate planning / elder law** — **the $15M exclusion and the sunset that never
happened** (top priority) · $19,000 annual exclusion · *Connelly* and
entity-purchase buy-sells · **WEP/GPO repeal** · portability 5-year election ·
Medicare 2026 figures · Medicaid asset limits (state-specific, verify individually)

**Bankruptcy** — **Subchapter V $7.5M sunset** and the lapsed combined Ch. 13
limit (top priority) · April 2025 threshold table · *Purdue* third-party releases
· CFPB medical-debt rule vacated · CTA/BOI obligations gone for domestic entities

**Criminal defense** — *Chatrie* geofence warrants · *Case v. Montana* emergency
aid · *Ellingburg* restitution · *Fernandez* compassionate release · *Rico* no
fugitive tolling · *Rahimi* § 922(g)(8) · *Erlinger* ACCA jury findings

**Family law** — *Rahimi* and firearm surrender in DV protective orders ·
**WEP/GPO repeal changes the math on dividing public pensions and evaluating
spousal Social Security in divorce** · 2026 poverty guidelines and SSA figures in
support calculations · state guideline updates (verify per state)

**Consumer / debt defense** — CFPB institutional instability · medical debt rule
vacated · click-to-cancel vacated, ROSCA live · *McLaughlin Chiropractic* (FCC
TCPA interpretations not binding) · *Cantero* national bank preemption

## 6. Audit heuristics

1. **Every bare dollar figure needs a source and an as-of year.** Without one it
   is presumptively stale.
2. **Flag temporal language:** recently, new, just, this year, currently, as of
   [year ≤ 2025], upcoming, will take effect, is scheduled to, pending appeal,
   expected to.
3. **Maintain the kill-list.** FTC noncompete rule, 2024 DOL overtime rule, CFPB
   medical debt rule, click-to-cancel, PWFA abortion provision, EEOC harassment
   guidance gender-identity sections, CTA domestic BOI, EO 14026, EO 14160.
4. **Treat *Chevron*, *Humphrey's Executor*, "background circumstances," "moment
   of threat," "third-party release," and "nationwide injunction" as presumptively
   wrong** without a post-decision qualifier.
5. **Require two-part SOL statements** wherever the state changed its period.
6. **Require escalating caps to be stated as a schedule**, not a single number.
7. **Distinguish "struck down" from "never took effect."**
8. **Check whether a sunset actually happened.** The two largest errors of this
   period run in opposite directions: the estate-tax sunset that **didn't**
   happen, and the Subchapter V and CCP § 377.34 sunsets that **did** and that
   copy still ignores.
9. **"Average settlement" and "we've recovered over $X" figures** are not legally
   stale but are a bar-rule exposure. Route them to `bar-advertising.md`.

## 7. Known gaps — do not assert these

- U.S. Reports **volume numbers** for opinions after roughly March 2026.
- **Holdings** for several OT2025 cases where only the date and docket were
  verified — restate a holding only after reading the opinion.
- FY2026 inflation-adjusted **USCIS** amounts in FR 2025-20622.
- A verified list of **public laws enacted in 2026** — congress.gov blocks
  automated retrieval, so mark any 2026 statute UNVERIFIED until checked.
- **Georgia SB 68** provisions on premises liability, seat-belt evidence, and
  negligent security.
- Colorado's **intermediate-year (2026, 2027)** med-mal cap figures.
- Federal **court of appeals docketing fee**, and the statutory components of
  district and bankruptcy filing fees.
- The exact **Subchapter V sunset date** (June 21, 2024 — widely reported, not
  pulled from primary).
- Statutory text of **Cal. Civ. Code § 3333.2** and **CCP § 377.34** — the 2026
  MICRA figures rest on a secondary source that matches the AB 35 arithmetic.
